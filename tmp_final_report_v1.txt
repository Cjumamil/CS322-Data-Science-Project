Abstract
We present a quantitative day trading system that combines exponential mov-
ing average (EMA) bands, moving-average convergence/divergence (MACD) mo-
mentum analysis, and pullback-continuation pattern recognition to identify high-
probability entries in 5-minute US equity data. The bot evolved over two academic
semesters from a naive SMA crossover baseline into a multi-filter confluence entry
pipeline with a slope-based MACD failure exit, deployed live on the Alpaca paper-
trading API. Backtests across a multi-asset universe—Microsoft (MSFT), NVIDIA
(NVDA), Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), and the ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK),
with Albemarle (ALB) used as a longer-horizon historical sandbox—show consis-
tent positive expectancy on trending names and confirm that relaxing a prior trend-
alignment gate on April 19–20 improved activity and returns across every tested
symbol (NVDA +0.61%→+0.95% / 669→933 trades, MSFT +0.15%→+0.26%
/ 154→211 trades, AMD−0.96%→ −0.57% / 381→503 trades; recorded in
RESEARCH LOG.mdentry R-002 and theshared backtests/* trend alignment sweep summary.json
artifacts). A pooled XGBoost entry filter was implemented and evaluated against
the plain strategy over a walk-forward holdout; although it modestly reduced aver-
age drawdown (−0.0909%→ −0.0762%), it underperformed the plain baseline on
combined profit factor (1.0828 vs. 1.1601) and was thereforenotpromoted for live
paper trading (R-003). A complementary VWAP + RSI mean-reversion strategy
was added (R-004), tuned on NVDA tomax bars in trade= 15 for a one-year
result of +0.2751% / PF 1.5873 / 127 trades (R-005), and promoted into a sep-
arate Alpaca paper account for a controlled live side-by-side comparison against
the MACD pullback baseline (R-006). Live paper trading exercised long entries,
short entries, MACD-failure exits, take-profit exits, and time-stop exits, validating
every coded pathway on real market data. The report documents the strategy ar-
chitecture, implementation, data results, and the honest limitations of the system,
including regime dependence, sector concentration, and modest absolute profitabil-
ity.

1 Introduction
In the fall of 2025, as we began Data Science 322, we asked ourselves a question that has
captivated traders and engineers for decades:Can a machine learn to trade better than
its designers?The answer, we discovered, is not a simple yes or no, but rather a nuanced
journey of discovery, failure, iteration, and hard-won insight. Our team set out to build
an algorithmic day trading bot, leveraging real-world APIs and historical market data.
We chose the Alpaca brokerage platform for both its paper-trading sandbox and its IEX
historical-bar feed, and used Python as the execution engine. What began as a textbook
SMA crossover strategy—elegant in its simplicity—soon revealed itself to be inadequate.
The markets demanded more sophistication, more nuance, morestructure. This report
follows a narrative arc while remaining grounded in quantitative evidence. Section 2
describes the strategy architecture and code organization. Section 3 presents empiri-
cal results across multiple securities, time horizons, and the live paper-trading window.
Section 4 discusses the key turning points in our strategy evolution, operational chal-
lenges including legacy artifacts, MACD exit redesign, and trend-alignment relaxation,
and reflects candidly on overfitting and sector-concentration risks. We also describe an
out-of-sample evaluation of a pooled XGBoost entry filter that we ultimately chosenot
to promote (R-003), the addition and tuning of a VWAP + RSI mean-reversion strategy
(R-004, R-005), and the dual-account live comparison setup that began on April 23, 2026
(R-006). The core insight that reshaped our entire architecture was this:The best trades
are not trend-starts, but pullback re-entries within a confirmed trend.Translating that
insight into code, filters, and disciplined exits is what this report is about.Team:Cooper
Jumamil, Ulyses Gonzalez, Joshua Truong, Havannah.Project duration:September
2025 – April 2026.

2.6 Modular System Architecture
The codebase is organised into functional modules (full layout in Appendix A):
•main.py: live event loop; polls Alpaca on a 60-second cadence (poll interval seconds)
for positions and bars. Order-status re-checks after submission use a separate 2-
second poll (order status poll seconds).
•backtest.py: offline event-driven engine replaying historical OHLCV pulled from
the Alpaca IEX feed.
•data.py: Alpaca bar aggregation and request normalization.
•broker.py: order-submission wrapper with shortability/ETB checks.
•execution.py: entry/exit signal logic.
•risk.py: position sizing, stop-loss placement, and portfolio exposure cap.
•state.py: trade state machine (entry, management, exit).
•strategies/: pluggable strategy classes (sma crossover.py,macd pullback.py,
macd pullback xgboost.py,vwap rsi mean reversion.py).
•xgboost filter.py,train xgboost filter.py: pooled ML entry-filter research.
•bot config.toml: editable TOML configuration shared across backtest and live
runs.

3.4 Pooled XGBoost Entry-Filter Evaluation
Between April 15 and April 21 we implemented a pooled XGBoost entry filter (see
train xgboost filter.py) using three feature variants—pooledsymbol only,pooled context only,
andpooled hybrid—trained across the active ten-symbol set. Training window: Jan 3,
2023 – Dec 31, 2024. Validation: Jan 1 – Sep 30, 2025. Out-of-sample test: Oct 1, 2025 –
Apr 21, 2026. Threshold selection occurred on the validation window; test results use full
filtered-strategy backtests, not just label classification. The best validation variant was
thepooled symbol onlymodel with a probability threshold of 0.50. On the held-out test
window, however, the plainmacd pullbackbaseline remained stronger overall (Table 7).
The filter did reduce worst-symbol drawdown (from−0.2375% to−0.2207%), and helped
AAPL, AMZN, META, MSFT, and QQQ, but it hurt AMD, ARKK, IWM, NVDA, and
SPY. The improvement was therefore not broad enough to justify promotion for April 22
paper trading, and the pooled filter remains a research artifact.

3.7 Backtest-to-Live Gap
Even with the expanded live sample, trade activity remains much sparser in live mode
than in backtest. The contributors we identify are:
1. Regime mismatch between April 2026 live conditions and the 2021–2025 backtest
corpus.
2. Polling-based data retrieval (60 s main-loop poll) versus event-driven bar-close as-
sumption in the backtester.
3. Bid/ask slippage relative to the backtest’s close-to-close fill model.
4. Intra-bar dynamics: 5-minute aggregates may produce a signal that looked valid on
close but has already been partially consumed by the time the live order fills.
These are discussed further in Section 4.

4 Discussion
4.1 The Journey: Key Turning Points
With hindsight, the project unfolded in four distinct phases.Phase 1 (Sept–Oct 2025):
The Crossover Era.We implemented a textbook SMA(20, 200) crossover on Alpaca
IEX data. It was elegant, easy to code, and consistently unprofitable. Whipsaws in
sideways regimes dominated the P&L. The lesson was humbling: simple is not always
better.Phase 2 (Nov–Dec 2025): The EMA-Band Hypothesis.We shifted to an
EMA band, added pullback detection, and discovered that entering during priceweak-
ness—not strength—materially improved win rate and risk-adjusted return. This was the
conceptual breakthrough that reshaped the architecture.Phase 3 (Jan–Mar 2026):
Parameter Tuning and MACD Integration.We refined the EMA window (com-
paring 50, 72, 100, and 200), integrated MACD histogram for momentum confirmation,
and began backtesting at scale. The April 7 sweep confirmed that the 72-bar EMA out-
performed the 200 on NVDA with pullback logic, and that a 3-bar pullback-breakout
lookback out-performed stricter values of 5, 8, and 10. The ALB sandbox was held at
k= 200 for continuity; NVDA, MSFT, and AMD were migrated tok= 72.Phase
4 (Apr 2026): MACD Failure Redesign, Trend-Alignment Relaxation, ML
Negative Result, and Dual-Strategy Live Comparison.On April 14 our initial
crossover-based MACD exit was replaced with the slope-based failure exit (D-032, D-033),
immediately improving exit timing in backtests. On April 19–20 a second conceptual re-
vision relaxedrequire recent trend alignmenttofalse(R-002, D-036), which lifted
NVDA returns from +0.6120% to +0.9511% and MSFT from +0.1546% to +0.2560% on
matched windows. On April 21 a pooled XGBoost entry filter was evaluated and explicitly
notpromoted (R-003, D-037), and a new VWAP + RSI mean-reversion strategy (R-004,
D-038) was scaffolded in research mode; a follow-up NVDA sweep that same day pinned
max bars in trade=15 as the cleanest tuning improvement (R-005, BT-011). On April
23 the project moved past a single live strategy: R-006 promoted the tuned VWAP/RSI
configuration into a second Alpaca paper account, so that the MACD pullback base-
line and the mean-reversion strategy now trade the same ten symbols side-by-side, with
independent broker-facing state.
4.2 Implementation Challenges and Debugging
4.2.1 Legacy “EMA200” Column Naming
Early iterations used a 200-bar band; when we migrated the live baseline to 72 we kept
theEMA200 *column names in the indicator DataFrame for backward compatibility with
serialized state and logging. The computation is correct, but the name is misleading
during audits. We document the artifact explicitly in code comments and in this report
and plan to refactor the column name post-submission.

Because slope is a derivative, it reacts to momentum decay one to two bars earlier,

across our test universe.
4.2.3 Trend-Alignment Gate (April 20 Relaxation)
The earlier version of the strategy required the same-direction trend state toremain
aligned throughout the pullback. Chart review on NVDA and AMD surfaced repeated
cases where the pullback damaged the trend state momentarily, yet the subsequent re-
covery bar was clearly tradeable. Relaxing the gate (R-002, D-036) improved all three
tested symbols:
•NVDA: return +0.61%→+0.95%; trades 669→933.
•MSFT: return +0.15%→+0.26%; PF 1.17→1.21; trades 154→211.
•AMD: return−0.96%→ −0.57%; PF 0.72→0.87; trades 381→503.
The conceptual lesson: a pullback setup can still be valid provided the recent trendcontext
is clear and the day is not sideways, even if perfect trend alignment is not preserved
through the pullback itself.
4.2.4 Missing Backtest Artifacts
An operational honesty note: the NVDA, MSFT, and AMD numbers in Table 4 are
drawn fromRESEARCH LOG.mdentry R-002 rather than from savedsummary.jsonartifacts
underbacktests/. During the April 20 parameter sweep we relied on the CLI sweep
mode’s printed output and failed to persist the per-run summaries. The numbers are
internally reproducible from the config values recorded in the research log plus the current
bot config.toml, but a reviewer attempting bit-exact verification of those three rows
would need to rerun them. We flag this as a reproducibility gap rather than a claim that
the numbers are incorrect.
4.2.5 Pooled XGBoost Filter: Negative Result
The pooled XGBoost filter is an instructive negative result. Validation selected a low
probability threshold of 0.50 on a symbol-aware (but symbol-only-featured) model, sug-
gesting the learner was anchoring to symbol priors rather than robust cross-symbol setup
quality. On out-of-sample test, the filter reduced drawdown marginally but lost more
alpha than it saved (profit factor 1.08 vs. baseline 1.16, per-symbol return 0.012% vs.
0.038%). We chose to ship the plain strategy into April 22 paper trading rather than a
filter that had not earned its keep.

4.2.6 Alpaca API: Polling, Shortability, and Preflight
Several operational concerns surfaced during live trading. Alpaca paper-trading en-
forces a shortability/ETB list; broker-side checks inbroker.pyreject short attempts
for non-shortable symbols. The bot polls the Alpaca API on a 60-second cadence
(poll interval seconds) and re-checks order status every 2 seconds after submission
(order status poll seconds), which is adequate for a 5-minute-bar strategy but would
be limiting at faster cadences. A startuppreflightreport (D-034) validates environment,
keys, and the symbol list before the event loop starts, and per-symbol exception isolation
prevents a single malformed bar from taking down the whole bot.
4.3 Risk Analysis and Limitations
4.3.1 Potential NVDA Overfitting
Most baseline parameters were first optimised on NVDA, a mega-cap growth stock with
strong intraday trending behaviour. AMD’s persistently negative back-test performance
(under both configurations) and MSFT’s dependence on the April 20 gate relaxation
together suggest the baseline may be partially over-fit to NVDA’s regime. A future
extension should walk-forward tune across a basket to reduce this bias.
4.3.2 Regime Sensitivity
Pullback-continuation strategies assume trending market structure with transient pull-
backs. In choppy, mean-reverting regimes the same pullback signature becomes a trap.
The strategy lacks an explicit regime detector (ADX, realised-volatility band, or market-
profile classification) and will mis-trade range-bound days. The VWAP + RSI mean-
reversion research path is motivated in part as a complementary regime tool.
4.3.3 Slippage and Execution
Backtests assume fill at bar-close prices. The live ARKK trade on April 16 filled at
entry$77.43 and exit$76.977 for−0.585%; the April 20 ARKK short round-trip (78.702
→77.76) captured a take-profit, but bid/ask spread, order-queue position, and intra-bar
movement all consume edge in production. A production-grade version would incorporate
a slippage model during backtest.
4.3.4 Sector Concentration
Our active symbol set is dominated by US large-cap technology (AAPL, AMD, AMZN,
META, MSFT, NVDA) plus broad-market ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM) and one thematic
growth ETF (ARKK). Correlated drawdowns during a tech-sector sell-off would hit the
whole portfolio simultaneously.
4.3.5 Modest Absolute Profitability
The 3-year ALB back-test grossed≈$508 on$100,000 (+0.51% cumulative, roughly
+0.17% annualised). NVDA’s +0.95% over five years is better but still below a riskless
Treasury return. The strategy’s edge is real but small; deployment with live capital would
require either leverage, much larger notional, or meaningful transaction-cost reduction.

4.3.6 Small Sample and Live Data
A few dozen live order events on each paper account is a diagnostic sample, not an
evaluative one. The short MSFT Q1-2025 backtest (seven trades) was correctly flagged
as statistically uninformative even before the April 20 promotion expanded it to 211
trades. The R-006 dual-account live comparison was set up specifically so that, as more
live data accumulates, both strategies are evaluated on the same underlying market path
rather than on cherry-picked replay windows.
4.4 Strengths of the Approach
1.Multi-layer entry confluence.Requiring trend, pullback, momentum, and ses-
sion filters to align simultaneously materially cuts false signals compared with single-
indicator systems.
2.Slope-based MACD exit.Reacting to therateof momentum change rather than
its level captures exits earlier than a zero-crossing rule.
3.Pluggable architecture.The codebase supports multiple strategies side-by-side;
macd pullback,macd pullback xgboost, and the newervwap rsi mean reversion
share the same risk, order, and logging scaffolding.
4.Honest ML evaluation.The XGBoost filter is an example of a disciplined nega-
tive result: it was built, evaluated with walk-forward validation, andnotpromoted
because the numbers did not justify it.
5.Live paper-trading integration.The early MACD pullback paper account ex-
ercised every coded exit path (long, short, take-profit, MACD failure, time stop),
and since R-006 the parallelvwap rsi paperaccount extends that coverage to the
mean-reversion code path on live data.
5 Conclusion
We have developed and evaluated an EMA-Band MACD Pullback Continuation strat-
egy for algorithmic day trading on US equities. The system shows conceptually sound
behaviour through positive multi-asset backtests (ALB 3Y: 303 trades, +0.5086% re-
turn, PF 1.2556; NVDA 5Y under the April 20 baseline: 933 trades, +0.9511%, PF
1.1224; MSFT 5Y: 211 trades, +0.2560%, PF 1.2068). It also stood up a complementary
VWAP/RSI mean-reversion strategy that, when tuned tomax bars in trade=15, pro-
duced the strongest single one-year run in the project (NVDA: 127 trades, +0.2751%, PF
1.5873, max DD−0.0936%) and is now running in a separate Paper2 Alpaca account for
a controlled live side-by-side comparison against the MACD baseline. Live paper trading
exercises every coded pathway (long, short, take-profit, MACD failure, time stop, and
end-of-day flatten) on real market data. Key takeaways:
•Pullback continuation out-performs pure trend-following in our hands; entering
during weakness within a confirmed trend is the dominant source of edge.
•MACD slope is superior to MACD crossover for exits; the April 14 redesign im-
proved exit timing and reduced late exits.

Recommendations for future work:
1. Add an explicit regime detector (ADX or realised-volatility-based) to gate strategy
selection.
2. Expand the tuning universe to multiple sectors (e.g. financials, healthcare) and
employ walk-forward optimisation to reduce NVDA-centric bias.
3. Integrate slippage and commission models directly into the backtester to reduce the
backtest-to-live gap.
4. Archive every promoted-config backtest run’s summary artifact so R-002-style re-
sults are byte-exactly reproducible from the repo alone.
5. Revisit the pooled ML filter with per-symbol calibration, stronger walk-forward
validation, and features that capture trade quality rather than symbol priors.
6. Promote the VWAP + RSI strategy to paper trading only after a broader cross-
symbol sweep confirms robustness beyond NVDA.
7. Move the decision loop from 60-second polling to Alpaca WebSocket streaming for
tighter latency.
Back-tests are backward-looking; live markets are unpredictable. The bot is a disciplined
tool, not a crystal ball. We consider the system ready for expanded paper trading under
the current baseline, and explicitlynotyet ready for live capital without further risk
controls, sector diversification, and cost-inclusive validation.

•Profit Factor: Gross profit divided by gross loss; a value above 1.0 indicates a

•Shortability / ETB: Alpaca’s designation of whether a stock is easy-to-borrow

•Slippage: Difference between expected fill price (e.g. bar close) and actual execu-

•Slope (MACD): Change in MACD value over a fixed window, indicating momen-
